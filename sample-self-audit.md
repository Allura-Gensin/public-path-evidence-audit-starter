# Sample self-audit: Allure Labs homepage

Reviewed August 12, 2026. This is a real review of an Allure Labs-owned public
homepage, not a client case study. It demonstrates the evidence format without
claiming lost revenue, a failed conversion, or improved results.

## Review brief

- Property: Allure Labs-owned homepage
- Question: Can a visitor identify the right next action?
- Surface: One unauthenticated desktop retrieval
- Boundary: Visible copy and public links only

## Finding 1

**Observation:** The hero offered two visible next steps: open ReviewPing or
send an email. It did not name a separate route for a visitor seeking operations
or workflow-review help.

**Why it matters:** A visitor interested in the broader operating-workflow
promise had to infer that email was the intended path.

**Bounded recommendation:** Add one plainly labeled service path, distinct from
the ReviewPing product route, if Allure Labs wants direct service inquiries.

## Finding 2

**Observation:** The homepage described operations and follow-up tooling as
“In exploration” while presenting ReviewPing as the current product.

**Why it matters:** The status distinction was clear, but there was no next
step for a visitor who wanted a bounded service conversation rather than a
future product.

**Bounded recommendation:** Publish a factual, fixed-scope service card only
when its scope, monitored inquiry route, and delivery boundary are ready.

## Finding 3

**Observation:** The public navigation exposed Products, About, and ReviewPing,
but no dedicated service or audit destination.

**Why it matters:** A relevant navigation path can reduce reliance on a visitor
scrolling and interpreting general company copy.

**Bounded recommendation:** Add the service destination after the service page
is live; otherwise keep the simpler navigation.

## What was not tested

The review did not inspect analytics, log in, enter data, submit a form, make a
purchase, access private systems, or test security controls.
